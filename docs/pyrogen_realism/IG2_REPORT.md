## 0. Summary

1. **Part 1 (why 4.8x Keller).** At the first-lit cells the sim's h is 2.1-3.3x a plain smooth developed-pipe
   Gnielinski value. The excess is four multiplicative terms: property-ratio K x1.5 (x2.2-2.8 at the
   cold wall), entrance x1.3-1.6, roughness (Haaland f at 32 um) x1.36-1.51 at Re 5e4-9e4 (x0.9-1.05 near
   transition), blend x0.93-1.0. Three of the four sit outside what the in-repo sources can support
   (Section 1.2); the Gnielinski property-ratio range itself is **pending R8**.
2. **Part 2 (closure).** Chosen: **Peretz, Caveny, Kuo & Summerfield (1973), Eq. III-22**, a bore
   starting-transient correlation. It is 3.4x (4.5 g) / 2.5x (0.45 g) below the sim's current h at the
   first-lit cells; Keller Eq. 11 (bore-scaled) is 4.8x / 4.5x below it and 1.4-1.9x below Peretz. Both are
   opt-in: `ignition_heat_transfer='peretz'|'keller'`.
3. **Part 3 (DeMar).** The 69.4 cal/cm^2/s is **not a measured flux**: it is
   `0.238 * W * M / t_A / (2 pi R L)` with W the impetus, M the charge, t_A the time to peak pressure, and an
   effective area built from a thermocouple distance R and length L that the slides do not give. One number
   per pyrogen, from one 0.56 L sealed vessel, no time record. **Recommendation: DROP it**
   (`heat_delivery_mode='none'`, no new code); no time shape is justified.
4. **Part 4.** The fix removes the "ignites with ~10-170x too little igniter" symptom (min charge 0.35 -> 1.4 g
   head basket, 0.028 -> 2.2 g forward plenum) but, exactly as IG1 predicted, **does not touch the
   realistic-charge over-spike** (18.5-27 MPa vs plateau 8.2 for every 4.5/6 g BPNV/MTV combo): the peak
   sits at pyrogen burn-out. Canonical-motor traces are unchanged (peaks within 1-4 %) when DeMar is kept;
   when DeMar is dropped **none of the three canonical motors lights at its Sutton-sized charge**.

## 2. Part 2 -- the chosen closure

**Peretz et al., "The Starting Transient of Solid-Propellant Rocket Motors with High Internal Gas Velocities",
Princeton AMS Report 1100, NASA Grant NGL 31-001-109, April 1973** (A. Peretz Ph.D. thesis;
`docs/references/peretz1974.pdf`, NTRS 19740005393; the AIAA J. 11(12)
journal version is not in the repo, R10). Read from the page images (the text layer drops the equations):

- Eq. III-22 (p. 38): `Nu_d,af = a6 (x_r/d_h)^-0.1 Re_d,af^0.8 Pr_af^0.4 (A_p/A_t)^0.4`, with a6 = 2.56e-2 (p. 40).
  Eq. III-31 (p. 40) is the same law in h-form: `h = 1.56e-3 cp Pr^-0.6 W^0.1 (p u/R)^0.8 T_af^-0.67 (x_r d_h)^-0.1 (A_p/A_t)^0.4`
  (check: (0.87e-6)^0.2 x 2.56e-2 = 1.57e-3, `tests/test_ignition_heat_transfer.py`).
- Properties at the film temperature T_af = (Tg+Ts)/2 except velocity; rho_af = p/(R T_af) (III-26);
  mu = 0.87e-6 W^0.5 T^0.65 poise for 1000-3500 K (III-24, fit to Svehla's air values); Pr = gamma/(1.77 gamma - 0.45) (III-25).
- Basis: a Dittus-Boelter form (III-21) with the entrance factor from Humble/Cholette/Bartz-type fits, the
  property treatment, and an A_p/A_t factor "deduced" by Peretz from Cohen-Derr-Price's reading of the Rocketdyne
  Carlson/Seader/Wrubel igniter-duct data (A_p/A_t 1.1-3, x/d <= 10; his a6 is set so III-27 equals their
  correlation III-30 up to x/d = 10; Eq. III-28 to III-30, refs 50-53, 158). **It is therefore a literature
  synthesis anchored to others' duct data, not a direct h measurement**; Peretz validated the whole model against his
  own measured pressure-time-space traces (his motor: A_p/A_t 1.06-2.0, ~40 atm, 11.5 g/s igniter; Table 4:
  Re_d 1.1e4-2.1e4 and Mach 0.28-0.58 during induction, to 4.5e5 aft-end during filling) and states "good agreement ...
  using Eq. (III-31)" (p. 41). Pre-ignition h applies to the propellant wall (p. 41).
- Why chosen over the alternatives: it is the only candidate built for exactly this problem (starting
  transients, head-end igniter, bore flow, local-property coupling), is in Nu-Re form so it uses the sim's
  local gas state, and it is the middle of the three: 3.4x below the current chain and 1.4-1.9x ABOVE Keller.
- Implementation notes (`_peretz_heat_transfer_coeff`): x_r = max(x, d_h); Nu floored at 3.66 (same
  laminar floor as the default chain); A_p/A_t = local cell port area / instantaneous throat area, used as
  published (no clamp; Chunc's 0.56 factor is x0.79 and is outside Peretz's range, flagged); gas R, Cp, gamma are
  the per-cell mixture values.
- **Keller-Baer-Ryan 1966 Eq. 11** (`keller1966.pdf`, DOI 10.2514/3.3678; Tg 1000-2600 K, 14-25 atm, 50-800 m/s,
  6 % average deviation; 0.500 x 0.125 in channel, D_h 5.08 mm; the paper attributes the steep G^0.905 to early
  transition from free-stream turbulence): `h = 1.435e-4 Tg^0.3 G^0.905 cal/cm^2 s K`. Implemented as a
  *lower-bound* alternative with the only dimensional extrapolation the form allows: h ~ G^0.905 D^-0.095
  (Nu ~ Re^0.905 at fixed properties), i.e. a (D/5.08 mm)^-0.095 factor; the property difference between
  Keller's N2/air and the bore gas is NOT corrected (the paper gives none) and Tg is the local static temperature.
  This is a stated extrapolation of a channel-specific fit (Section 5.1).
- Both modes change ONLY the h that heats the unignited surface (and its matching convective wall-loss
  sink) in the Goodman kernel. The Ma 2020 erosive path (`burn_rate.py`) is untouched and not modified.

## 3. Part 3 -- the DeMar flux (`STARTING YOUR UP-GOERS_pub (1).pdf`, TRATECH 2021; `probes/ig2_demar_energy.py`)

What the 69.4 cal/cm^2/s is (slides "Parameters from test data" and "Explanation of the derived measures"):

- Apparatus: 5.75 in x 2.75 in ID aluminum tube (0.56 L, sealed), 1000 psi transducer in the forward closure,
  **one** K-type thermocouple "in closer proximity to the igniter", dipped/molded igniter on a nichrome head.
- Directly measured: peak pressure, time to peak pressure, thermocouple temperature (BPNV: 105 psi, 67 ms, 840 C).
- **Derived** "rough estimate of *relative* heat flux": `q = kE W M / t_A / (2 pi R L)`, kE = 0.238 cal/J, W the
  impetus (J/g), M the pyrogen mass, t_A the time to peak pressure, L an "effective igniter length", R the thermocouple
  distance. R and L are **not given**. Back-solving the tabulated rows (my inference, M = P V/W = 0.72 g for BPNV)
  gives an effective area 2 pi R L of ~21 cm^2 (BPNV), 19 cm^2 (BKNO3-NC), 17 cm^2 (MTV): different per row.
- So 69.4 = (the total gas-expansion energy of a ~0.7 g charge) / (the 67 ms time to peak) / (a geometry-dependent
  area): a **mean power per reference area over the closed-vessel pressure-rise window**, in a vessel that has no
  propellant surface. It is not a measurement of heat flux into a surface, it carries no time record (the
  slides contain only the table and a closing remark "1/2 gram of dipped BPNV, ~60 ms action time"), and it is
  one number per pyrogen.
- It is not intrinsic to the pyrogen: by DeMar's own definition it scales as M/(t_A A). Applied to the sim's
  4.5 g charge that burns out in ~9 ms the same formula gives ~50x the table value. The sim instead imposes the
  vessel number as a fixed step on the head cell (capped by the pyrogen's sensible-enthalpy flow).
- Energy ledger (probe, 0.1 s runs): the DeMar flux delivers only **0.65-3.9 J** to the grain versus 1.1-10 kJ of
  pyrogen gas enthalpy (6e-5 to 3e-3 of it), so the issue is not double-counted energy; it is that
  an unconditional 2.9 MW/m^2 step on one cell sets the forward-plenum ignition threshold
  (Section 4.1: 0.028 g).

**Conclusion: a time-dependent form is NOT justified.** The only definitional relation available preserves the
average only for DeMar's exact configuration (0.72 g, 67 ms, his geometry); any time shape for a 4.5-6 g
basket would be an assumed shape fitted to an average, which the user's condition excludes. **Recommendation:
drop the DeMar flux** and let ignition come from the modelled convective channel (Part-2 h) plus
radiation where already modelled. No new code: `Pyrogen.heat_delivery_mode='none'` (this is what the 'off'
rows use). R11 resolved (user, 2026-10-11): the slides are the whole DeMar source; no time record exists.

## 5. Caveats

1. **Keller is channel-specific** (D_h 5.1 mm, shock-tube free-stream turbulence, early transition) and the sim's lowest-G cells
   (G ~30 kg/m^2s = 3 g/cm^2s) are below its ~9 g/cm^2s lower estimate (G itself is not tabulated); it is included as a
   bound, not as the chosen closure.
2. **Peretz's A_p/A_t factor and a6 come from Rocketdyne duct data at A_p/A_t 1.1-3**; Chunc's first-lit cells have 0.56 (x0.79 on
   h). Removing the factor would raise Peretz h by 1.27x and lower the thresholds accordingly (not run; would need a code variant).
3. The sim's Re uses a constant mu (Peretz film-T mu gives 2.2x higher Re); this is shared with the erosive path and
   is not touched here.
4. Ignition time scales roughly as 1/h^2, so the 3.4x lower h maps to ~11x later first light at the same flux; measured first
   light moves 3-4x (0.66 -> 2.1-2.5 ms) because the gas also heats and pressurizes the bore during the delay.

## 6. Recommendation (the user decides)

1. **Ignition h:** adopt `'peretz'` as the ignition closure default. On the canonical motors it changes
   only first-light time (1.7-2.8 ms -> 5.8-7.7 ms; peaks within 3 %), it is the literature-grounded choice for this regime,
   and it removes the 4.8x-over-Keller excess. Keep `'gnielinski'` selectable. (Staging: ship opt-in first; flipping the default
   moves the first-light time of every motor and re-baselines B0 unless done together with the re-record.)
2. **DeMar:** drop it (`heat_delivery_mode: none` in the library YAMLs), because it is a derived reference-vessel number,
   not a flux, and a time shape cannot be justified. **Do not drop it before the igniter default is revisited**:
   with Peretz + DeMar off the library Sutton charge fails to ignite Chunc (0.90 vs 2.2 g), BALLSstick (1.39 vs 7.2 g) and
   Hasegawa A (4.18 vs 14.4 g). Either require an explicit charge, or size the default from the (now meaningful) ignition
   threshold. The head-basket topology is unaffected by the DeMar decision (threshold 1.43 g either way).
3. **Over-spike:** not addressed by A (peak/plateau 2.2-3.3 at 4.5-6 g in every combo); go to option B (pyrogen burn law,
   needs R1-R3, R5) and the erosive amplification (held).
